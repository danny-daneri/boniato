Taft-Hartley Act:
I EMPLOYER FINANCED UNION HEALTH AND
WELFARE FUNDS
Section 302 of the Talt-Hartley Act places several
unnecessary, but not seriously crippling, restrictions on
payments by employers to union welfare funds.
Time Table:
Welfare funds established prior to the enactment of
this Act need not be brought into compliance until termi-
nation of present contract, or July i, 1948, whichever
occurs ecrlier.
(Sec. 302 f)
Welfare funds established prior to January 1, 1946,
are not allected by any provision of this section. (Sec.
302 g)
Any fund that provided for pooled vacation benefits
would be illegal alter July 1 or the end of the present
contract, whichever occurs earlier, except those which
were entered into prior to January 1, 1947. (Sec. 302 g)
Welfare plans paid for and administered by either
management alone or labor alone, are not affected.
is only when management pays money to the union for
such purposes that this section applies.
Restrictions:
The company can no longer agree to pay any money,
to the union welfare fund (if entered into after January
1, 1946) unless a written agreement is made with the
employer detailing how such welfare payments are to
be made, and providing equal representation of manage-
ment and labor in the administration of such fund, with
provision for neutral parties or umpire to settle disputes.
if neutral parties or an umpire can not be agreed upon,
the United States district court shall name the umpire.
The agreement must provide for an annual audit and
public posting of results.
The usual welfare benefits are still permitted with the
exception of pooled vacation benefits (if established after
January 1, 1947). However, pension funds must be kept
in separate trust and used only for pensions or annuities.
Penalties:
Violation of any of these provisions is subject to prose-
cution as misdememor, and subject to $10,000 fine and/or
a year in prison.
This section of the Act is not under
the jurisdiction of the N.L.R.B. Action is taken directly
to United States district court.

Adjusting Plans to Tait-Hartley Requirements:
In the first place, joint responsibility for handling funds
of this nature is not necessarily a handicap. Some unions
prefer such arrangements since accusations of misuse
of funds can not be made against the union officials by
dissident members.
In the second place, joint representation on the trust
fund does not mean joint representation in detailed
administration of the wellare program or in the purchase
of insurance covering members.
This is very significant because in many states there
is no enabling legislation that will permit a trust fund
to purchase insurance policies. There is nothing in the
Tait-Hartley Act that prohibits the trust fund from author-
izing the remittance of money from the trust fund directly
to the union, which in turn will have sole responsibility
for purchasing the insurance and handling all claims
and disputes with the insurance company directly.
Some unions have set up their own direct service
health centers and cash benefit plans without any outside
insurance or benefit agencies involved at all. The admin-
istration of these direct service plans can still remain
exclusively in the hands of the union so long as money
received from employers is first turned over to the jointly
administered trust fund, which in turn authorizes remit-
tance of funds to the union welfare department or health
center.
It is probably sate to assume that the trust fund could
not tum over unquestioned amounts of money to the
union. The payments should be made to the union wel-
fare department or health center on the basis of detailed
agreements and with provision for detailed auditor's
report to the trust fund.
I. UNION AUTHORIZATION ELECTIONS IN
SEASONAL INDUSTRIES
Unions in seasonal food packing industries should
watch carefully for the results of two union shop authori-
zation cases presented by the Teamsters for two citrus
packing plants.
(Cases:
McKeon Canning Co., Inc.
No. 21-UA-48 and Winckler & Smith Citrus Cos. No. 21-
UA-143).
Under this Act, seasonal industries employing large
groups of transient workers are in a bad enough situa-
tion just with simple representation elections.
If the
N.L.R.B. abides by past precedent and holds elections
at or near the peak employment period, the exclusion
from the unit of temporary supervisors and the necessity
of showing 30% representation will be difficult problems.
Now the N.L.R.B. is faced with making the decision as
to when union shop authorization elections can be held
... in the peak... or in the slack period, when only
a small percentage of year-round employees are em-
ployed. They are struggling with the question of whether
a union shop authorization election held in the slack
period could apply to seasonal employees hired in
peak, or whether the election would have to be held at
the peak if so applied.
Even if the N.L.R.B. could solve the above questions,

To the Membership of The International Association of Machinists -
Greetings:
Ever since the passage of the Taft-Hartley Law members of labor unions
throughout the length and breadth of this country have been subjected to a
constant barrage of propaganda favorable to the Act.
Senators, congressmen,
employers, editors, columnists, and commentators have burst forth, in print
and on the air, in praise of the law.
Similar propaganda - some of it spon-
sored by the National Association of Manufacturers - is also being widely dis-
tributed to employed men and women at their places of work in industry.
Concentrating their attention on selected individual features of the
Taft-Hartley Lar, the self-styled friends and advisors to labor have tried in
a cunning and subtle way to convince the average union member that the sole
purpose of the Act is to protect him from power-drunk labor officials. Never
in the history of my long experience in organized labor have the elements
opposed to labor unions ever attempted to mislead the workers and all the plain
people of our country on such an extensive and well-organized scale.
In order to get at the facts, the IAM has prepared the following state-
went which attempts to present the real meaning of the new labor law to organ-
ized labor both with respect to the Act itself and to the propaganda of those.
who are so eagerly trying to defend it.
In view of the great importance of this matter to members of organized
labor and all rorkers, it is essential that this information be conveyed to
the greatest number in the shortest possible period of time.
I, therefore,
request that you pass this on to your fellow workers or neighbor as soon as
you have finished with it. Additional copies of this statement may be obtained
from the International Association of Machinists, Machinists Building,
Washington 1, D. C.
Fraternally yours,
H. W. Brown
INTERNATIONAL PRESIDENT
