RE: MONOGRAPH ON TAFT-HARTLEY ACT
Gerhard P. van Arkel, former General Counsel of the National Labor
Relations Board, has just completed for the Institute a new monograph
providing a thoughtul and provocative section by section analysis of the
Taft-Hartley Act. It is now on the press and will be available in approxi-
mately two weeks.
Under the title "An Analysis of the Labor Management Relations
Act, 1947", Mr. van Arkel has packed into 75 pages his observations con-
cerning the implications of the changes in the old Wagner Act which this
new legislation has created, the areas of ambiguity and conflict, and the
sign-posts for probable interpretations which can be detected in the legis-
lative debate that preceded the Act, or which have thus far arisen in
its administration.
Mr. van Arkel's unique qualification as the author of this monograph
need not be emphasized. I sincerely believe he has produced by far the
most authoritative and soundly analytical commentary on the Act yet to
appear. It bears directly on the problems which are arising daily in the
confusion and uncertainty of labor-management relations and litigation
under the impact of the new law. It is, I believe, as essential a part of the
library of all those who are engaged in, or who will engage in labor work,
as the text of the Act itself.

it sought to effect reforms through economic and industrial
organization, rather than through political organization.

Unions to resolve themselves into political organizations

Governments and by the fortunes of political parties.

Where is the Unions' Constructive Program?

MONG the songs that enjoyed a
great vogue a yeat ago was a
sprightly little number called "Ac-

Probably the best job of ear-stuffing was

lack of affirmative goals is particularly dispir-
iting at a time when the worldwide survival
of free institutions appears to depend almost
as much on the luminosity of our political
example and morale as on the abundance of
our material aid.
