"PIPER CALLS FOR PAT"
All labor and all management must now commence to "pay the piper" for
the tune he has been called to play by those few in both groups whose hunger
for power 1e greater than their logic. It has been evident for Jears that
voluntary methode of arbitrating the settlement of industrial disputes worked
- and worked well - when both groups set their minds and hearte to the job.
It has been equally clear that if their example in the use of voluntary aroi-
tration were not followed - especially by those who run corporations and labor
unions in key industries - the public would "call the cop" and try to bring
peace by force.
The railway strike cocked the gun. The coal strike pulled the trigger.
State legislatures and the Congress are going great guns. Scores of bills have
been poured into the legislative hoppers.
The State legislatures are also generally in session. And out of their
hoppers commence to come the pay-off for the piper of indifference, neglect and
repudiation of the voluntary arbitrations which have characterized too many of
the reconversion disputes.
These are obviously aimed mainly at John L. Lewis and stem clearly from
public resentment of his complete repudiation of arbitration:
But the roots of the present situation lie deeper than the acts of any
one man.
They grow out of a fundamental question which both labor and manage-
ment must resolve together. That question is:
Can we work out together a mutually satisfactory voluntary
method for determining what is just and equitable when our re-
spective interests seem to clash, or
do we prefer a government bureau to settle these disputes by
"compulsory arbitration" and inevitably assume the corollary
power to write our contracts for us?
The two functions are Siamese twins. Take one and you get the other willy
nilly. Regrettable though it will be, the innocent will suffer along with the
guilty if even a few powerful groupe pursue a course of action that leads the
country's lawmakers to the conclusion that labor-management relations are beyond
self-regulation by the parties through the mutual use of voluntary arbitration.

ASSOCIATION PROGRESS
1947 marke the beginning of the second decade of maintaining special voluntary
Labor Arbitration Tribunals in AAA. Prior to 1937 labor cases were handled by the
same technical pereonnel as commercial cases. Growth has been phenomenal. In 1946
as many labor grievances were arbitrated as in the first 5 years of AAA. January
and February indicate that 1947 will far exceed 1946 in the use that unions and em-
ployers will make of the nation wide facilities afforded them by AAA. In flexibility,
economy and competence these facilities are exceptional. In industry after industry
the major companies and unions are finding through experience that AAA Rules are a
major protection against abuse, delays and excessive costs. They find that arbitra-
tors selected from AAA panels render the highest type of impartial service with the
"know-how" to make awards that are clear, just, and helpful in creating and maintain-
ing a good mutual relationship.
That of course is the sole objective of AAA. Free from political or any other
special influence, labor and management have jointly succeeded in creating in the AAA
Voluntary Labor Arbitration Tribunals an instrument for self-regulation that is the
nation's strongest bulwark against the growing tide of political ambition to secure
control through the settlement of disputes.
Whatever differences arise between management and labor in the future, they
have created in AAA one enterprise that proves they can successfully cooperate in
settling their family disputes by fair, efficient and modern methods, through private,
non-political, impartial, and voluntary arbitration.
