TO ALL LOCAL UNIONS, GREETINGS:
Our Union and each INDIVIDUAL MEMBER of it is at the present time confronted with one of the most
serious problems which we have faced in several decades. At the present time there is legisintion before
Congress which will set back our Union and INDIVIDUAL MEMBERS fifty (50) years.
The history of our nation will prove that fair legislation is not enacted quickly. Our predecensora
In the Trade Union movement fought for many years to obtain the present legislation which protecte
our job opportunitles and our individual welfare. This legislation was not easily enacted, but was ob-
tained only through diligent effort.
I include herewith, copy of a letter sent by this office to all the Representatives and Senators who are
now considering this legislation.
"In 1846, the Glass Bottle Blowers Association was founded and authorized to bargain col-
lectively for its membership in the United States and Canada. This Trade Union recognized its
one hundredth anniversary in the year just past.
Since 1885, there has not been a general strike in the glass container industry. Likewise,
national bargaining has been carried on since the year 1885, with great success to both the
industry and the members of the Glass Blowers' Union.
The glass container industry is one hundred percent organized under Union Shop Contracts
which include all of the advanced thinking in modern relations, such as the Check-Off, Seniority,
Overtime Protection, and many, many other fair labor standards.
During the war years our industry, through the cooperation of our Union, was able to
increase production from fifty million (50,000,000) gross in 1940 to one hundred and fifteen
million (115,000,000) gross in 1946. We helped immeasurably to keep the general public and
our Armed Forces supplied with the necessary containers which protected the health and wel-
fare of our nation. This record is most outstanding and was based on total cooperation and
collective bargaining without interference by Governmental Agencies or others who know not
the intricate problems confronting the glass industry.
The present Congress has before it a series of labor bills which will not promote industrial
peace, but which will retard the progress made by this great nation. Free Enterprise has been
the keynote in recent election campaigns. Free Enterprise for whom?
We appeal to the sound thinking citizens who are our Representatives in Congress to vote
against legislation which is conceived hurriedly and passed in a spirit of ill will. Our nation
economically must move forward and certainly the enactment of anti-labor legislation will con-
tribute much to our present economic standards being lowered.
Your vote against this proposed legislation will permit our forty thousand (40,000) members
to continue their progress of honesty and sincerity in dealing with our Manufacturers.
Trusting we will have your cooperation,
LEE W. MINTON, President."

It has become evident that the 80th Congress knows not what it
is doing nor where it is going. The 80th Congress is harbor-
ing an illusion believing that the 1946 election results were
specifically directed toward the annihilation of the rights of
free men.
We believe that the time has come when Labor must make itself
heard in the halls of Congress. Appreciating, of course, that
your good offices have done everything in their power to
bring
about a complete understanding as to what is necessary in order
to continue our democracy as we know it and enjoy it.
We, of course, are advocates of free enterprise and free labor,
but the time has come when the rights of free men are being
challenged. We find that today, big business, through the
National Association of Manufacturers, is drawing strategic
battle lines directed explicity against organized Labor.
We
further find that the National Association of Manufactuers is
directing all of its propaganda to the unorganized for consump-
tion.
We feel that Labor's first counter-reaction should be in
the field of public relations, i.e.: What Labor stands for, its
policy and program, its responsibility to our Government, its
struggle for existence in elevating the working man's standards
which made this country the great country we know it to be.
