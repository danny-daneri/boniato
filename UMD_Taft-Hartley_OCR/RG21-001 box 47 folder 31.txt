FACT SHRET
On Pending Anti-Labor Legislation
1. Motivation
The sponsors of this legislation claim Congress has a mandate
from the American people to pass labor* legislation, They base thie
claim on the results of the 1946 election. Undoubtedly the voters were
disturbed in that election by postwar shortages, the slowness of re-
conversion, the failure of Government controls and recurring labor-
management strikes. The people were sick of war on the industrial
front. They wanted peace and production.
Taking advantage of this "mandate", the anti-labor crowd
are now trying to cram down the public's throat legislation actually
Intended to destroy free trade unionism in America. This is not a
program for labor-management seace, but for complete chaos in the
nation's industrial life. As the debate progresses, the proponents of
this legislation are coming out from behind their hypocritical mask and
exposing their real motives. Rep. Fred Hartley, Chairman of the House
Labor Committee, said during the House debate on his bill (which was
later adopted by an almost 3 to l vote):
"The purpose of this legislation is to break the unions down
to the local level."
Senator Robert Taft, Chairman of the Senate Labor Committee,
said on the opening day of debate on the Senate Bill:
A few days later he told the American Newspaper
Publighers Association in New York City that the bill
includes many of the matters "which were strenuously pressed
upon us by employers."
Thus the cat is being let out of the bag. Do the
American people believe that the way to labor peace and more . :.
cooperative labor-management relations lies in crippling the power of
the trade unions to serve their 15 million members effeotively?

A FEW QUESTIONS AND ANSVERS.
What is wrong on the industrial front?
Why all these strikes?
What are the facts, the rights and wrongs?
1, Any fair minded study will show that labor-management
strife is the exception rather than the rule. Wherever management
accepts its legal duty to bargain sincerely with the union representing
1ts employes, trouble disappears. It 18 only when management continues
aggressive warfare against unionism and resists efforts of the workers
to get their fair share of the rewards of increased production that
war breaks out. Such war is provoked -- deliberately in many cases -
by employers. Yet for every strike there are 999 other cages of new
contracts agreed upon around the bargaining table without loss of
production.
2 - True, 1946 was a record-breaking year for strikes.
Why? The answer is obvious. Postwar maladjustments, which afflicted
every phase of the nation's economy, had to be ironed out. Long-
postponed increases in wage rates, to make up for loss of wartime
take-home pay, were refused to labor by management in many instances
until and unless the Government granted price increases. This was
true in many cases where management could have absorbed increased
labor costs without increasing prices. Management's policy was
directed not only at resisting profit-sharing through wage adjustments
but at breaking down the whole system of Government controls.
3 - No one person knows all the facts and all the
answers.
That is why President Truman urged Congres in 1946 to create
a fact-finding investigation before attempting to pass labor
legislation Labor is not afraid of the facts.
The American Federation of Labor endorsed this
proposal.
But Congress spurned
the idea and passed the anti-labor Case Bill, which the President
vetoed. Again this year, the President urged Congress to authorize
a fact-finding investigation. Again Congress is ignoring his wise
advice.
Instead it is rushing headlong into the worst legislative
blunder in United States history.

AN ANALYSIS OF SECTION 8 OF H. R. 3020 AS PASSED BY THE SENATE
OUTLAWING THE SO-CALLED CLOSED SHOP
The seven and one-half million members of the American Fedor-
ation of Labor are divided up into approximately forty thousand
local unions affiliated with the National and International Uniona
or dirootly affiliated with the American Federation of Labor.
Four million membors of these forty thousand local unions are
now working under union-shop contracts. In a number of instances
union-shop contracts are entered into between the Employers'
Associations and the National or International Unions covering the
affiliated local unions on an industry, regional, or local basis.
Most of the local unions, have business representatives and
maintain offices, which is the focal point for their membership.
Day-rooms are maintained where unemployed members can congregate
daily for the purpose of finding employment. The business repre-
sentative acts as the counselor and employment agent.
The practices referred to herein would be wiped out completely
by H. R. 3020 as passed by the Senate. Employers would be
prohibited from making agreements that would permit this practice
to continue. In its stead, provisions are made for union-shop
contracts where anyone may be hired with a provision that they join
the union in 30 days and where a majority of workers affected vote
for same, under the supervision of a National Labor Relations
Board. This, in itself, is a highly impractical feature, one that

today a union may enter into a union-shop agreement with a large

It is inconceivable that a bureaucracy could be created that

The bill further provides that the union under a union-shop

To the Offioers of National and International Unions,
State Federations of Labor, City central Bodies and
Directly Affiliated booal Unions.
Dear Sirs and Brothers:
The Executive Council of the American Federation of
Labor at its recent meeting in this city gave special considera-
tion to the drive which is being made against organized labor
through the attempt to enact anti-labor legislation.
in state legislatures and in the Congress of the United States
° na sh tho
This drive
is being given the full support of the National Association of
Manufacturers and other anti-labor groups and organizations.
The vicious anti-labor bill sponsored by Congressman
Hartley passed the House of Representatives. by an overwhelming
majority. It seems indeed inconceivable that Congress would
pass such a measure.
It is destructive of the rights as well
as the individual and collective freedom of the workers through-
out the nation.
It revives government by injunction; makes some
strikes illegal; provides for the institution of damage suits
against unions for alleged violation of contract; prohibits
secondary boycotts; prohibits foremen from belonging to unions;
provides for the destruction of closed-shop agreements and
industry-wide bargaining; outlaws joint welfare funds; drasti-
cally changes the United States Conciliation Service; provides
for drastic amendments to the National Labor Relations Act; and
empowers the President to seize industries essential to the
public welfare, as the mines were seized under War Powers.
An analysis of this anti-labor measure shows that it
will be very difficult for trade unions to function at all under
the provisions of this reprehensible anti-labor legislation.
In an effort to combat this anti-labor legislation
the Executive Council decided to launch a nation-wide newspaper
and radio campaign. This campaign has been launched. A full-
page advertisement appeared in newspapers in the cities through-
===== PAGE 50 =====
out the nation on Monday, April 28th. This will be continued
for some length of time.
Arrangements have been made for the
launching of a radio campaign.
All of this is designed to
educate and acquaint the people of the nation with the dangers
which threaten the basic freedom and liberty of the workers of
the nation, as well as all classes of people, through the enact-
ment of anti-labor legislation.
The Executive Council authorized the expenditure of
substantial suns of money in this newspaper publicity and radio
campaign. Through this method we will carry on our fight in a
vigorous, militant and aggressive way against this slave
legislation which is being enacted by the Congress of the
United States.
State Federations of Labor, Central Labor Unions and
all other organized units of the American Federation of Labor
can help in a most valuable way in this campaign.
You can help
by having the individual members of organized labor and their
friends in the respective communities write their United States
Senators and Members of the House of Representatives protesting
the enactment of this legislation and calling upon them to vote
against it.
Perhaps you can supplement this by having petitions
signed and forwarded to the Members of the House and Senate.
Our enemies have organized against us and are carrying
on a Vigorous campaign in behalf of this anti-labor legislation.
We, too, must organize in opposition to this fight
which is being made against our very existence.
This is your fight; the fight of all who cherish the
principles of freedom and democracy. In complying with the
urgent request we are herewith making, you will be fighting
for yourselves, for the enjoyment of liberty for yourselves
and for your families.
Because of the seriousness of the situation we
respectrully urge all the members of organized labor to do
their part by responding to the appeal we have herein made
to communicate with their representatives in the House and
Senate of the United States immediately.
Fraternally yours,
President,
American
Federation of Labor
