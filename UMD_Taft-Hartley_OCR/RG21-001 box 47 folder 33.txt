Dear President Green:
The Taft-Hartley bill has now become the
"Labor-Management Relations Aot, 1947". It 1s a
good time to recall, as Professor Benjamin Selekman
has said, that a mature person faces the facts of
life as
something to be handled rather than something
to be hated.
In all the conflict of discussion, one basic
fact stood firm -- indeed was underscored:
That the
solid core of American management and labor agree in
preferring industrial self-government with freedom of
compulsion of a strike or lockout.
This basic choice is the characteristic feature
of the democratic experiment that lies ahead of us.
The
endurance of western civilization probably depends
upon
the degree of our success. We can succeed only by the
unswerving loyalty of responsible management and labor to
this fundamental choice.
I suggest that this choice means that not only
strikes and lockoutsbut also the resort to government
interposition under the expanded jurisdiction of the
NLRB
and Its General Counsel, or any other form of imposed
settlement, mustbe thought of by both labor and management
as a last resort.
