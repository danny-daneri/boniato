The Commercial Telegraphers' Union, ATL, representing 50,000 communications
workers in the United States is gravely concerned over the present agitation to
rush through drastic legislation to "curb" labor. Coviously this agitation stems
from the series of strikes and lockouts which occurred last year, but it is also
receiving the encouragement and support of all of the labor-hating, anti-union
Individuals and groups in the country who favor such legislation irrespective of
whether or not it is warranted.
The strikes and lockouts of last year were the result of dislocations in the im-
mediate post-war period which were abnormal beyond any previous experience.
They
were to be expected, if not inevitable,
following the most devastating war in all
history.
The most remarkable thing about them is that they were not more serious.
They offer no justifiable basis for the proposed legislation.
Labor legislation pending before Congress, particularly the "Ball-Taft-Smith
Bill", would deprive labor of the right to strike and create a new widespread
system of Government controls as a substitute for free and genuine collective
bargaining.
We believe that is the road to chaos, more and more Government con-
trols, eventual totalitarianism.
Perhaps some "corrective" legislation may be necessary.
Labor's responsibilities
have increased tremendously with greatly expanded organization in the past de-
cade, perhaps to a much greater extent than many of us in Labor realize.
We must
fully recognize and assume these responsibilities and discharge their attendant
obligations for the good of the Nation. We want to do so. We do not want to be
deprived of that opportunity and right by "punitive" legislation.
We believe the logical solution to this problem is that proposed by President
Trunan: a patient, exhaustive study of the problems involved to determine in-
telligently what if any legislation is necessary. That would be the practical,
realistic approach.
